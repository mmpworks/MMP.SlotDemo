Dolphin Splash PAR Analysis

An analytic model of the SpinPlay "Service Test Math.xls" workbook, what it reproduces, and the full hunt for the one number that resists: Hit Chance 0.41552. Source scripts: python/spinplay-par/. Workbook copy: assets/spinplay-par-source/.

1. What the model is

The analyzer reads the workbook and rebuilds the game math from first principles. It enumerates every stop combination on the real reel strips. It uses zero random spins, so its numbers carry zero sampling error. A simulation only converges to these values as the spin count grows [Hannum p.5, law of large numbers]. The counting model is Muir's "hits over cycle": the cycle is the product of the strip lengths, an event's probability is its hits divided by the cycle, and RTP is the sum of prize × hits over cycle × bet [Muir ch2]. The same sum is Hannum's wager expected value per unit bet [Hannum p.3].

2. The reels are drums, and there are two sets of them

Two facts about the strips matter to every number below.

Each reel is a drum. A strip of N symbols has N stop positions. The 3-row window at stop i is strip[i], strip[(i+1) % N], strip[(i+2) % N]. The last two windows wrap past the end of the list back to the top. A walker that stops at the end of the list sees only N−2 windows and shifts every probability. The analyzer wraps; section 5 shows what happens when a walker does the opposite.

stop i: [ s[i], s[i+1], s[i+2] ] middle of the strip stop N-2: [ s[N-2], s[N-1], s[0] ] wraps once stop N-1: [ s[N-1], s[0], s[1] ] wraps twice

The workbook ships two strip sets. The base game and the free-spins game spin different drums. The analyzer reads both (columns A–E and G–K of the Reel Strips sheet) and uses the feature set for every feature figure: the retrigger rate, the single feature spin RTP, and the feature RTP all come from the feature cycle.

3. What the analyzer reproduces

Seven of the eight Game Summary figures reproduce to all five published decimals. One rule had to be inferred to get there: the sheet's Paytable lists the wild's own row as zero, but the RTP only reproduces when a pure-wild run pays as the best symbol it can stand for. That is the "wild pays highest" rule, applied through Muir's prioritisation discipline: under "highest win pays", one line pays one best rule [Muir ch2].

4. The one open number

The Game Summary sheet says Hit Chance is 0.41552, described as "The chance of getting any wins". Three observations frame the hunt.

Hit Rate is the same fact. The sheet's Hit Rate cell is 2.407, and 1 ÷ 0.41552 = 2.4066. The two cells are one number in two costumes. The workbook offers no second clue.

The sheet contradicts itself. The Lines cell says 1, and the Programming Notes say the math is "based on single line only". A single line on these strips hits once in 31.6 spins (0.03161). A hit rate of one in 2.4 belongs to a ways-style game. Both claims cannot describe the same game.

Every other cell derives from the strips. Cycles, scatter hits, trigger chance, and the RTP set all reproduce by enumeration. Hit Chance is the only Game Summary value with no visible derivation.

The union bound

One bound removes a whole family of guesses. Every payline is a path that picks one row per reel. The union of ALL left-to-right path evaluations over every possible line therefore equals the ways union: a symbol counts on a reel when it sits anywhere in that reel's window [Muir ch3 models ways games this way]. On these strips that union is 0.40162. The sheet's 0.41552 sits above it. So no left-to-right line scheme, with any number of lines, can produce the sheet's value. Whatever produced 0.41552 counts something else.

The families, briefly. Path schemes are rule variants inside left-to-right evaluation; the union bound caps them all at 0.40162. Walk/window rules break the drum on purpose: they walk the strips the way a buggy tool would, so the denominator and every window count shift. Tallies count wins instead of winning spins, so coinciding wins push them over the union. Fold-ins put free-spin hits into the count: the expected free spins per base spin (0.0837) come from the trigger weights and the retrigger series. Line family is the sheet's own Lines=1 claim, tried four ways.

6. Where that leaves the 0.41552 cell

No constructed rule lands on the cent. The two closest are the flat walk (0.41050) and the flat walk folded over all spins (0.41472). Both contain the same ingredient: a walker that treats the strip as a flat list and drops the wrap windows. That is the drum mistake, made by the sheet's tool rather than ours.

Simulation noise covers the rest of the gap. At 100,000 simulated spins, a probability near 0.4155 has a standard deviation of about 0.0016 [Hannum p.6, volatility guidelines]. The flat fold-in sits half a standard deviation from the sheet's cell; the flat walk sits about three. A sheet author who measured hit frequency from a modest simulation run, on a tool that walks strips flat, would very plausibly print 0.41552.

The working conclusion: the cell was measured or inherited, and it cannot be derived from these strips by any left-to-right rule. The question is with the sheet's author. Until the answer arrives, the RTP figures stand verified and the Hit Chance cell stays flagged as unreproducible.
